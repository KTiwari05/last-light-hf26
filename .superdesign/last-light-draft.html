The Uninvited Frequency

The house settled with a sigh that wasn't its own. Elias stood in the corridor, the silence of the hallway light extinguishing behind him felt heavier than the dark itself. It was as if the absence of light had weight, a physical pressure pushing against the small of his back, nudging him toward the rooms that should have been empty.

He felt the change before he heard it—a sudden, sharp bloom of frost against the back of his neck. The kitchen, usually smelling of stale tea, now carried the metallic scent of a winter that hadn't arrived yet. In the corner, where the floorboards didn't usually creak, something shifted. Not a footstep, but the distinct pressure of someone shifting their weight on a chair that wasn't there.

The radio flared to life, not with a broadcast, but with a rhythmic, pulsing static that matched his own heartbeat. He stared at the study door, waiting for it to swing open, but it remained stubbornly, terrifyingly shut. When the hallway light finally flickered back on, the brightness felt like a scream, revealing nothing but the dust motes dancing in the sudden, lonely glare.
